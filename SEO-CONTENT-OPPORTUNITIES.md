## Evidence and publication gates

1. **Existing site versus new site:** obtain approved examples of both commissioning options, integration responsibilities and a real scope checklist. Do not invent cost savings or imply one option is universally better.
2. **Film workflow:** obtain an actual script/source-note example and responsible clinical approval. Discuss the studio process; publishing medical explanations requires appropriate sources and a real clinical reviewer.
3. **AI presenter:** confirm actual provider/usage/consent/deletion arrangements before describing technical or contractual guarantees. Use genuine authorised footage, never a generated person presented as a real clinician.
4. **Format adaptation:** obtain actual media permissions and show what was changed. No growth, engagement or lead statistics without measured evidence.
5. **Treatment directory:** use the approved project scope and page structure; obtain permission for further annotations or quoted client comments. Clearly distinguish design rationale from measured performance outcomes.

Each published guide should answer its main question promptly, explain the examples, link to the relevant commercial service, credit real authors/reviewers and use actual publication/revision dates. Add Article data only after an actual article exists with those facts.

Until the evidence exists, keep these as proposals. The current site already answers basic fit, cost/scope, approval and platform questions on the appropriate service page; those short answers do not need separate thin articles.
